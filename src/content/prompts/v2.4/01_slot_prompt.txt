INSTRUCTIONS FOR THIS CONVERSATION

SUGGESTED SETTINGS: temperature 1; maximum output 20,000 tokens; extended thinking with a budget of 16,000 tokens.

YOUR TASK

You prepare the inputs for the last phase of a temporal thought experiment. A temporal thought experiment takes an accepted theory and one relationship in it, imagines a temporal condition that the theory never stated, and asks what would happen to the relationship if that condition held. It has four phases. A researcher has completed the first three and recorded each in a table:

Select: the researcher chose the theory, put six questions about time to every relationship (the audit), and drew one cell of a temporal list (a dimension of time at a scale), a conception of time and one relationship R.
Explore: the researcher represented the theory as a table of constructs and relationships and generated candidate changes to R, one change each.
Transform: the researcher reasoned from each surviving change to a mechanism, checked the mechanism against four criteria, and selected one statement: under temporal condition C, R becomes R′, through mechanism M.
Provoke, the phase you prepare: a language model, in separate conversations, proposes settings in which C could hold, writes two accounts of the chosen setting with synthetic survey tables, and reports what it knew at its training cut-off. Those prompts are general. They read every value specific to the run from a block of slots, and you write that block from the researcher's tables.

Work in two steps. In step 1 you check the tables. In step 2, only if the tables are complete, you write the block.

STATIC TABLES (part of this prompt; the researcher does not supply them)

These tables are the same in every run. The researcher's tables refer to them.

Dimensions: Speed and tempo: how fast an activity is done or a change takes place. Duration and horizon: how long an activity, a state or an effect lasts. Sequence and order: the order in which activities or events occur. Rhythm, cycle and entrainment: the recurrence of an activity at a pattern of intervals; one complete recurrence; the adjustment of one rhythm to another, so that the two keep step. Timing: when an activity or event takes place, relative to the clock, to a calendar or to other events. Trajectory and path: the course that a quantity or a relationship follows over time.

Table S2. Conceptions of time, numbered in the order of the draw, each with its definition.
1. Clock time: time measured in uniform, divisible units, independent of the events that fill it.
2. Process time: time counted by events and activities, such as episodes of use, and not by the clock.
3. A-series time: time as past, present and future, in which events pass from future to past.
4. B-series time: time as the order of earlier and later, which does not change.
5. World time: time as people meet it in what they are doing, as the time for something.
6. Time as a resource: time as a scarce means that people allocate and spend.
7. Time as a structure: time as the schedules, cycles and patterns that order activity.
8. Time as a process: time as the continuing unfolding of events.
9. Temporal work: the work people do to construct and reconcile accounts of past, present and future.
10. Ambitemporality: holding more than one temporal orientation or conception at once.

Table S3. The audit questions, one for each dimension, put to every relationship. Duration: does the theory state the interval over which the relationship holds? Sequence: does it give an order among the constructs the relationship joins? Rhythm: does it name a cycle in which the relationship recurs? Timing: does it say when the effect is expected? Speed: does it say how fast the cause produces the effect? Trajectory: does it state the path the relationship follows over time? "No" makes the relationship eligible for the draw on that dimension.

Table S4. Relationship terms and admissible changes. Relationship terms: direct effect; indirect effect; mediator; moderator; interaction; threshold effect; omitted variable. Admissible changes to R: diminished strength; increased strength; change of sign; disappearance; introduction or removal of a mediator; introduction or removal of a moderator; a new path between constructs already in the theory.

Table S5. The four Transform criteria. 1. M is specific and interpretable in the theory's vocabulary and at its level. 2. R′ is detectable with established instruments. 3. Temporal check: M follows from C and would not arise without it. 4. C uses the words of the drawn cell of Table S1 and its scale.
